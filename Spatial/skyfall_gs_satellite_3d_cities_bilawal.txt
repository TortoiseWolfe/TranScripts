[0:18] The physics problem is quite simple. These satellites are largely looking oblique down or slightly off nadir. All that geometry is just occluded, which is fine if you can fly planes with camera arrays everywhere, but you can't. You've got aerial-denied regions, you've got restricted airspace, you have conflict zones. Huge chunks of planet Earth are literally unmapped with these methods, which is why you only have about 3,000 cities worth of 3D data inside of Google Earth.

[0:54] These researchers found a workaround that is honestly kind of beautiful. They train Gaussian splats on multi-view satellite imagery — basically standard 3D Gaussian splatting stuff. But naturally, as the camera descends towards ground level, the renders completely fall apart. You've got floating artifacts everywhere, this geometric nonsense.

[1:11] Now, instead of trying to fix this technically, they just leaned into it. They treat those broken renders as input into an image diffusion model like Flux and basically say, "Hey, hallucinate and fill in the missing parts." That nightmare street-level view becomes a starting point for diffusion to autocomplete, in a sense.

[1:31] But here's where it gets really clever. They generate multiple diffusion samples per view instead of committing to one solution that is probably wrong. If you generate a couple of versions and let Gaussian splatting optimization find consensus across them, you actually get multi-view consistency that looks pretty damn good. They do this refinement in episodes — this curriculum-style training starting at higher altitudes and gradually descending. Hence the name of the paper, Skyfall-GS.

[1:57] So with each iteration, those ground-level views get less screwed up and more refined. The diffusion model starts filling in the facades, adding the texture detail, fixing the artifacts. By the end, you've got real-time flyable 3D cities that look surprisingly convincing. And critically, the geometry still respects the satellite input. It's not just making stuff up — it is intelligently completing whatever was occluded. No 3D training data required, no street-level photos, no airplane flyovers, just satellite + diffusion doing what it does best: filling in the blanks.
