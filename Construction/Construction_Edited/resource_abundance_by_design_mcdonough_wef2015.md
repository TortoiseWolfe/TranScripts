# Resource Abundance by Design — William McDonough (WEF 2015)

Source: https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c
Speaker: William McDonough (architect & designer) · World Economic Forum · ~21 min

> A later talk on the same **cradle-to-cradle** framework as [the 2007 TED talk](./cradle_to_cradle_design_mcdonough_ted2007.md), but reframed around a sharper thesis — **be "more good," not just "less bad"** — and supported largely by *new* case studies (NASA Sustainability Base, Make It Right in New Orleans, refugee housing, "mining the cities," soil-toxicity data). See the **Comparison** section at the bottom for what's shared vs. unique.

---

## [0:07](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=7) Less Bad Is Not Good

- The frame: **resource abundance and design**, drawing on *Cradle to Cradle* and its sequel *The Upcycle* (with chemist Michael Braungart).
- **"Design is the first signal of human intention."**
- **"If we intend to be less bad, perhaps we'll still be bad — just less so, by definition. So what would it mean to be more good?"**

## [0:46](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=46) A 12th-Century Poem (Hildegard of Bingen)

- "Glance at the sun, see the moon and the stars, gaze at the beauty of the earth's greenings. Now think." — **Glance, see, gaze, think.**
- Critiques the word *sustainability*: if you described your relationship with a loved one as "sustainable," "I'd say I'm sorry." It doesn't sound interesting — we should aim higher than merely sustaining.

## [1:40](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=100) Born in Japan; Hiroshima

- Born in a traditional Japanese house; as a child heard oxcarts collecting sewage for the farms — **cities and farms as one organism** (also the Dutch model).
- At five, learned of **Hiroshima**: "a city that took thousands of years to build, took seconds to destroy." This drove him to study international relations — to understand how humans could do this to each other.

## [2:44](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=164) From Physics to Biology (E, M… and Biology)

- A physics professor sent him to special relativity: a huge C squared × a tiny M (one hydrogen atom) = the atom bomb. **"A tool's value is put there by the purpose to which the human puts it."**
- Staring at entropy, he looked for **negative entropy** and couldn't find it in the physics library — because, from a design perspective, **it's biology**: nature pulls CO₂ from the air into soil; the only real income is solar energy.
- **Francis Crick** (essay *The Nature of Vitalism*, 9 years after DNA): to be a living thing requires three conditions — **growth, income, and an open system of chemicals** operating for the organism and its reproduction. Therefore **mass is precious** — treat it preciously.

## [4:36](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=276) Buildings Like Trees — and Children Eating the Building

- Resolved that as an architect he would **design buildings like trees**.
- Designed a daycare in Germany that grows its own food, makes its own energy, provides community laundry. Realized **the children were physically mouthing/eating the building** — which forced the question: what is it *made of*? That sent him to find Michael Braungart.

## [5:36](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=336) Two Metabolisms; Aluminum

- Humility again: 5,000 years to put wheels on luggage — "we're not as smart as we sometimes think."
- **Biological nutrition** (returns safely to nature) and **technical nutrition** (returns to industry forever — polymers, aluminum). **Since 1850, ~75% of all aluminum humans have made is still in circulation.**
- Even solar collectors using cadmium can be "nutrition for technology of the future" — **as long as they're safe** and kept in the technical loop.

## [6:12](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=372) The Textile (8,000 → 38 Chemicals)

- A textile mill on a river in Indonesia ran black and toxic. They screened **8,000 textile chemicals down to 38** (for wool and ramie).
- Result: water leaving the mill into Lake Constance was **as clean as Swiss drinking water**; trimmings that had been hazardous waste (shipped to Spain for burial/burning) became **mulch for the local garden club**.

## [6:59](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=419) Carpet as Technical Nutrient

- The US discards **1.4 billion pounds of carpet per year**, some of it hazardous PVC.
- For Berkshire Hathaway's carpet company: face yarns → caprolactam, backing is thermoplastic polyolefin — **carpet becomes carpet forever**. You "store your materials on your customers' floors" (a **materials bank**) — a perpetual customer relationship.

## [7:38](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=458) Soil Toxification — Ecocide & China

- *Ecocide in the USSR* (read while writing the **Hanover Principles**): 16% of the former USSR rendered uninhabitable; arable land ~50% impaired.
- **China:** ~12% of land is arable; a government statistic declared **19.4% of that too contaminated to grow food** — cadmium and lead. Hunan-province rice "should not be eaten."

## [8:46](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=526) A Toxin Is a Material in the Wrong Place

- **"A toxin is a material in the wrong place."** Lead in a computer (solder) is useful; lead in a child's brain is brain death.
- **"When did we decide carbon was a toxin? We *are* carbon."** We turned carbon from a millennia-built asset into a liability by sending ancient carbon back into the atmosphere — "backwards, upside down" → the greenhouse effect. **"If it's not our design, who's got the plan?"**

## [9:37](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=577) NASA — Come Back to the Blue Planet First

- Asked by NASA to help design a Mars space station, he replied: **"Before we go to Mars, what if we come back to the blue planet first?"**
- With the space-station team (in the "Houston, we have a problem" room), designed a building in **Mountain View, California** (NASA Sustainability Base): makes **120% of the energy it needs** from renewables and **purifies its own water down to the last molecule** — on a normal federal office budget, ahead of schedule.
- "Let's not just talk about more efficient buildings. Let's talk about **buildings like trees** — and we want *more* of them."

## [10:34](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=634) Stop Reporting Badness; Increase Goodness

- Mocks "reduce emissions 20% by 2020" as aiming at *nothing*: like jumping in a taxi and shouting **"Quick — I'm NOT going to the airport."** It tells no one where you're going.
- Do **both**: remove the bad **and** increase the good. Every molecule vetted (no mercury in mother's milk), energy clean and renewable, water safe and healthy. **"Out with that stuff, in with this stuff" — constant improvement.**

## [11:37](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=697) The Upcycle & the Circular Economy

- **Upcycling: things get *better* every day, not just less bad** — and it sends children a positive vision of the future.
- Products going through **cradle-to-cradle certification** for the circular economy: materials as nutrients, **reverse logistics**, waste = food. **Reverse the "take-make-waste" paradigm.** "God never sent a bill for the trees" — imagine materials **cheaper than free**.

## [13:06](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=786) Mining the Cities; Struvite from Sewage

- **Either/or with an "e":** mining gold from the earth yields ~$210/ton; **mining circuit boards from old phones yields ~$7,500/ton.** So **mine the cities** — design today's products for what's next.
- A sewage-plant mechanic found pipes clogging with minerals; run through a vortex it precipitates **struvite (phosphate)** — a slow-release fertilizer sellable at 12% profit. Conventional sewage treatment "is silly": it chlorinates valuable nutrients and dumps them. Turn it into farmer fertilizer instead (we each need 3–4 g phosphate/day).
- Imagine Paris or Washington, D.C. with **rooftop farming** — cities growing their own food.

## [14:38](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=878) Ford River Rouge

- Revitalized Ford's **1,000-hectare River Rouge site in Detroit** with the **world's largest green roof** — makes oxygen, purifies water, and **saved $35M in CapEx on day one** vs. conventional chemical stormwater systems (equivalent to a $900M car order at 4% margin).

## [15:11](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=911) Make It Right — New Orleans

- After Katrina (10,000 displaced), McDonough and **Brad Pitt** founded **Make It Right**, building a neighborhood of 150 houses (≈103 at the time) with architects worldwide and resident choice.
- Children had returned from FEMA trailers with **asthma from formaldehyde**. The new homes: ~**$400 mortgage**, solar power, healthy materials — "the children came back. They couldn't breathe. Now they can breathe."

## [16:11](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=971) Netherlands — Buildings as Material Banks

- A Netherlands project makes its own food and power. Office buildings sold at the cost of ~one year's rent because people want to be there; **buildings as material banks** — **designed for disassembly**, storing materials for future generations (worth ~1.4% in the financing pro forma).
- A massive new project near **Schiphol**: a **cradle-to-cradle hub for the circular economy** (logistics + biology side by side).

## [16:58](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=1018) Refugee Housing — Sixth-Graders as Designers

- After Pakistan/Haiti disasters and a nudge from **Muhammad Yunus (Grameen Bank)**, took on housing for the "super poor."
- Reframes relief waste (sachets, packaging, NYC plastic films costing $80/ton to dump) as building material. His **design team was sixth-graders**: a house buildable **in a day, by children, without tools** — a dignified 10-ft core with addable wings (up to eight bedrooms), spring-flip windows, **mushroom insulation**, rivet-together polymer parts; styles left to the people.

## [19:23](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=1163) Venkataswamy — Generosity, Not Limits

- Commercial model inspired by **Dr. Venkataswamy** of Madurai, India: mass-produced cataract surgery dropped lenses from $300 to $2 and surgery from ~$1,900 to ~$20; those who could pay did, and **2 million people received eyesight for free.**
- This flips commerce from **"how much can I get for how little I give"** (limits) to **"how much can we give for all that we get"** — generosity, fairness, abundance.

## [20:59](https://youtu.be/uKlqL_nuh_c?t=1259) Closing — Glance, See, Gaze, Think, Then Do

- The light bulbs of the world, seen from space, "are fires." Everything we make is chemicals that **come back to the ground** — so stay "grounded in fundamental principles that drive our design."
- Returns to Hildegard: **re-see the world — glance, see, gaze, think.** Start with the children, think about the future, and then **"let us do. Because it's time for action."**

## Comparison with the TED 2007 Talk

Both talks present the **same cradle-to-cradle framework** by the same speaker; this WEF 2015 version reuses the spine but adds a thesis and mostly fresh evidence. Use the TED talk for the framework's first articulation and the WEF talk for the "more good, not less bad" sharpening and the later projects.

**Shared in both** (don't expect new info here):
- "Design is the first signal of human intention"
- Two metabolisms (biological / technical nutrition); waste = food
- 5,000-years-to-put-wheels-on-luggage humility line
- E=mc² → physics/chemistry/biology; Francis Crick's growth/income/open-system conditions
- The 8,000 → 38 textile-chemicals story
- Shaw / Berkshire Hathaway closed-loop carpet
- Dr. Venkataswamy's mass-produced eye surgery
- Ford River Rouge green roof saving $35M day one

**Unique to WEF 2015** (the reason to keep this transcript):
- The central **"be more good, not less bad"** thesis + taxi "I'm *not* going to the airport" analogy
- **The Upcycle** (the sequel book) and explicit **circular-economy / cradle-to-cradle certification** language
- Origin story via **Japan** (oxcarts/sewage) and **Hiroshima** (vs. TED's Hong Kong / Yale story)
- **"A toxin is a material in the wrong place"**; cadmium/lead as solder vs. in the biosphere
- **"When did we decide carbon was a toxin? We are carbon."** — carbon as asset vs. liability
- **NASA Sustainability Base** (Mountain View, 120% energy) — "come back to the blue planet first"
- **Make It Right** post-Katrina New Orleans (Brad Pitt, formaldehyde/asthma, $400 mortgage)
- **"Mining the cities"** (gold $210/ton vs. circuit boards $7,500/ton); **struvite** from sewage
- **Refugee/super-poor housing** designed by sixth-graders; build-in-a-day, mushroom insulation; Muhammad Yunus
- **Buildings as material banks / design for disassembly**; Netherlands & Schiphol circular-economy hub
- Soil-toxicity data: ***Ecocide in the USSR***; **China's 19.4% contaminated arable land**
- **Hildegard of Bingen** "glance, see, gaze, think" poem framing
- Critique of the word **"sustainability"** as too low a bar
