# Cradle to Cradle Design — William McDonough (TED 2007)

Source: https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo
Speaker: William McDonough (architect & designer) · TED2005 (published 2007) · ~21 min

> Green-minded architect William McDonough asks what our buildings and products would look like if designers took into account "all children, all species, for all time." The talk introduces the **cradle-to-cradle** framework: designing materials and buildings as part of closed nutrient cycles rather than a one-way path to the landfill.

> **Related:** McDonough gave a later talk on the same framework at the World Economic Forum (2015) — [*Resource Abundance by Design*](./resource_abundance_by_design_mcdonough_wef2015.md). It sharpens the thesis to "be more good, not less bad" and adds newer projects (NASA Sustainability Base, Make It Right, refugee housing). See that file's **Comparison** section for shared-vs-unique material.

## [0:26](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=26) Design Is a Problem We Can See

- Opens with Rachel Carson's *Silent Spring* (1962) — for makers of things, "the canary in the mine wasn't singing." The disappearance of birds became a fundamental question.
- A child's **rubber duck sold in California carries a warning** that it "contains chemicals known by the State of California to cause cancer and birth defects."
- **"What kind of culture would produce a product of this kind and then label it and sell it to children? I think we have a design problem."**

---

## [2:55](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=175) Design Is the First Signal of Human Intention

- Core premise: **"Design is the first signal of human intentions."** If we wake up with designs on the world, what is our intention as the now-dominant species?
- Reframes the stewardship-vs-dominion debate: each is implicit in the other ("how could you dominate something you had killed?").
- The guiding question for a designer is whether design can "secure local societies, create world peace and save the environment."

---

## [4:05](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=245) The Question Behind Every Design

- McDonough's firm uses **the tools of commerce** (quick, creative, efficient, and "fundamentally honest, because we can't exchange value for very long if we don't trust each other").
- The question they bring to every project: **"How do we love all the children of all species for all time?"**

---

## [4:16](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=256) The Strategy of Tragedy

- Modern culture appears to have **"adopted a strategy of tragedy"** — outcomes like global warming become our *de facto* plan because we have no other plan.
- On meeting federal agencies: "I pointed out that they appear to have no plan. If the end game is global warming, they're doing great."
- **Design humility:** "It took us 5,000 years to put wheels on our luggage." There is no endgame — quoting Kevin Kelly, "There is an infinite game, and we're playing in that infinite game."

---

## [5:37](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=337) The Cradle-to-Cradle Goal

The goal McDonough says he presented to the White House:

> **"A delightfully diverse, safe, healthy and just world, with clean air, clean water, soil and power — economically, equitably, ecologically and elegantly enjoyed."**

- Demands **full diversity** (the De Gaulle joke: "trying to run a country with 400 kinds of cheese").
- Names the non-negotiables: clean **water** (a UN-declared human right), clean **air**, and clean **soil** (citing nitrification and the dead zones in the Gulf of Mexico).
- On the energy transition: wind in the Great Plains is the first solar form to beat fossil-fuel hegemony. Quotes Sheikh Yamani: **"The Stone Age didn't end because we ran out of stones."**

## [6:20](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=380) Materials Down to the Parts Per Million

- "Our products are not safe and healthy" — so the firm **analyzes chemicals down to the parts per million**.
- Example: a Pendleton baby blanket designed so its materials contribute nutrition rather than harm.
- "Companies acting ethically in this world are outperforming those that don't."

---

## [7:19](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=439) Material Flows — Horror and Hope

- **The terrifying side:** a hospital monitor shipped from Los Angeles to China exposes a worker to toxic phosphorus and lead. Car steel can't become car steel again (coating contaminants — bismuth, antimony, copper) so it **downcycles** into building steel.
- **The hopeful side:** Dr. Venkataswamy in India mass-produces eye care and has given eyesight to two million people for free.
- With Berkshire Hathaway / Shaw Carpet, they developed a **continuously recyclable carpet**: Nylon 6 top returns to caprolactam; a polyolefin backing is an infinitely recyclable thermoplastic.
- The bird's-eye test: a conventional building is a *liability*; The Gap's campus with an ancient meadow is an *asset* — nesting grounds.

## [8:29](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=509) Where This Thinking Came From

- Grew up in **Hong Kong** — six million people in 40 square miles, four hours of water every fourth day in the dry season; learned nutrient flow from farmers who worked the same ground for 40 centuries.
- Studied at **Yale** in a Brutalist (Le Corbusier-style) building.
- On a sealed glass tower (Mies' 1928 design) rebuilt in Houston with windows that don't open: **"this is actually a vertical gas chamber."**
- As a student, built the first solar-heated house in Ireland — though a teacher, Richard Meier, insisted "solar energy has nothing to do with architecture." ("I guess he didn't read Vitruvius.")
- In **1984**, designed the first "green office" in America (for Environmental Defense). When he asked manufacturers what was in their materials: "They're proprietary, they're legal, go away."

## [10:12](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=612) "Away" Went Away

- The realization: there is no "away" to throw things to.
- NOAA's **Pacific Gyre** was dragged for plankton and yielded **six times as much plastic as plankton** — "kind of like a giant toilet that doesn't flush."

## [10:40](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=640) Use Something as Elegant as a Tree

- The book *Cradle to Cradle* "is a polymer. It is not a tree" — its first chapter is literally titled "This Book is Not a Tree."
- The design assignment nature already solved — **design something that:**
  - makes oxygen
  - sequesters carbon
  - fixes nitrogen
  - distills water
  - accrues solar energy as fuel
  - makes complex sugars and food
  - creates microclimates
  - changes color with the seasons
  - and self-replicates
- "Well, why don't we knock that down and write on it?" — the point being that we destroy the very thing whose design we should emulate.
- Beyond the usual criteria (Can I afford it? Does it work? Do I like it?) he **adds the Jeffersonian agenda: life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.**

## [11:53](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=713) Competition as "Strive Together"

- *Competition* comes from the Latin **competere — "to strive together"** — the way Olympic athletes train together, get fit, then compete.
- Reframes "survival of the fittest" not as destroying the other but as **fitting together, building niches, and having growth that is good.**
- On growth: environmentalists resist it ("asphalt = assigning blame"), but citing Francis Crick, **life itself requires growth as a precondition** — free energy, sunlight, and an open system of chemicals.
- So the real question is **not growth vs. no growth, but "What do you want to grow?"** Grow the things we'd enjoy, not destruction.

## [13:41](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=821) Two Metabolisms: Biological and Technical

Developed with German chemist **Michael Braungart**, two fundamental metabolisms:

- **Biological nutrition** — materials that safely return to the soil. Can only support ~500 million humans ("if we all wore Birkenstocks and cotton, the world would run out of cork").
- **Technical nutrition** — materials kept in **closed industrial cycles**, recovered and reused indefinitely. Must scale an order of magnitude beyond biological nutrition.
- Materials must be analyzed to the parts per million for cancer, birth defects, mutagenic effects, immune disruption, biodegradation, persistence, and heavy-metal content.
- **Proof of concept:** screening a textile, they filtered ~8,000 industry chemicals down to **38**. They then databased the 4,000 most common manufacturing chemicals and released it publicly so designers worldwide can vet their products.
- A **supply-chain protocol** carries these requirements upstream and downstream — because most companies only know their stuff comes from "suppliers" and goes to "customers."

## [15:12](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=912) Products in Closed Cycles

- **Biological nutrients:** the first fabrics — water leaving the process was clean enough to drink.
- **Technical nutrients:** Shaw Carpet — nylon → caprolactam → carpet, infinitely reusable.
- **Biotechnical:** the Ford Model U concept car ("cradle to cradle car"); Nike shoes with infinitely recyclable polyester uppers and biodegradable soles. "Wear your old shoes in, your new shoes out. **There is no finish line.**"

---

## [15:46](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=946) Buildings That Give Back

- **Oberlin College** building — makes more energy than it needs to operate and purifies its own water.
- **The Gap** building — roofed with the ancient grasses of San Bruno, California.
- **Ford's River Rouge plant, Dearborn** — the world's largest green roof at the time (10½ acres). The approach **saved Ford $35 million on day one** (vs. conventional stormwater infrastructure). The first species to arrive — **killdeer** — showed up in five days, and auto workers began learning bird songs.

## [16:38](https://youtu.be/IoRjz8iTVoo?t=998) Designing Cities — Cradle-to-Cradle Urbanism

- Cities are "the home of technical nutrients"; the country is the home of biological. The work is putting them together.
- Commissioned to design **12 cities for China** using cradle-to-cradle as templates — housing protocols for **400 million people in 12 years**.
- A mass-energy balance showed that building in brick would consume their soil and coal, leaving "cities with no energy and no food." China signed a Memorandum of Understanding to adopt cradle to cradle.
- **The design method for a new city:**
  - Study the **hydrology, ancient biota, current farming**, the **winds and sun** — so every apartment gets fresh air, fresh water, and direct sunlight at some point in the day.
  - Lay out **parks as ecological infrastructure** first, then building areas, then mixed commercial/residential use.
  - **Transportation:** everyone within a five-minute walk of mobility; a 24-hour street so there's always a place that's alive.
  - **Closed waste loop:** sewage treatment plants are sold as assets — they produce natural gas that powers the city's cooking; wastewater builds wetlands for habitat; compost goes to **rooftop farms** where the native landscape is lifted onto the buildings, with little bridges between roofs.
  - Solar power from the industrial zones' light-colored roofs powers the city. Ground floors are work/live space.

---

> **Closing idea:** Cradle-to-cradle design treats every material as a nutrient — either biological (returns safely to soil) or technical (returns to industry forever) — so that the built world, like a tree, gives more than it takes. "There is no finish line."
