## 1. Core Premise — Design Is Intention

- **"Design is the first signal of human intentions."** Every artifact reflects a choice about what kind of world we want.
- The guiding question McDonough brings to every project: **"How do we love all the children of all species for all time?"**
- Avoid the **"strategy of tragedy"** — outcomes nobody intended (e.g., pollution) become the *de facto* plan when there is no better plan. Design toward "an endgame of something delightful."
- Requires **design humility**: "It took us 5,000 years to put wheels on our luggage." There is no finish line — it's "an infinite game."

## 2. The Cradle-to-Cradle Goal

> A delightfully diverse, safe, healthy and just world, with **clean air, clean water, soil and power** — economically, equitably, ecologically and elegantly enjoyed.

Non-negotiables to design toward:
- **Clean water** (a UN-declared human right)
- **Clean air**
- **Clean soil** (avoid nitrification / dead zones)
- **Clean power** (solar/wind — "The Stone Age didn't end because we ran out of stones")
- **Full diversity** — ecological and cultural

## 3. Two Metabolisms

Every material should belong to one of two closed loops:

| Metabolism | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Biological nutrition** | Returns safely to the soil; biodegradable, non-toxic. Limited scale (~500M humans). | Fabrics whose process water is clean enough to drink |
| **Technical nutrition** | Stays in **closed industrial cycles**, recovered and reused indefinitely. Must scale ~10× biological. | Shaw Carpet: nylon → caprolactam → carpet |

- **Downcycling is failure**, not success: car steel becomes building steel because coating contaminants (bismuth, antimony, copper) can't be removed.
- Materials should be vetted **down to the parts per million** for cancer, birth defects, mutagenic/immune effects, persistence, and heavy-metal content. (McDonough's team filtered ~8,000 textile chemicals to 38.)

## 4. Use Something as Elegant as a Tree

A design benchmark — nature already builds things that make oxygen, sequester carbon, fix nitrogen, distill water, run on sunlight, create microclimates, and self-replicate. Ask whether a design *gives back* the way a tree does.

- **Buildings as assets:** Oberlin College building makes more energy than it uses and purifies its own water; Ford's River Rouge green roof (10½ acres) saved $35M on day one and attracted wildlife in five days.
- **Products without a finish line:** "Wear your old shoes in, your new shoes out."

## 5. Cities & Developments

When the framework scales to urbanism (McDonough's 12-cities-for-China commission):
- Study **hydrology, native biota, prevailing winds, and sun path** first — guarantee every dwelling fresh air, water, and daylight.
- Lay out **parks as ecological infrastructure** before buildings.
- **Closed waste loops:** sewage → natural gas (powers cooking); wastewater → wetlands/habitat; compost → rooftop farms.
- Walkability (5-minute mobility), 24-hour living streets, work/live ground floors, solar from industrial roofs.
