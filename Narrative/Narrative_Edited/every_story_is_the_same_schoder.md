# Every Story is the Same — Will Schoder

Source: https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA
Speaker: Will Schoder (video essayist) · published Nov 2016 · ~15 min · drawn from Dan Harmon's "Story Structure 101"

> A video essay arguing that every recognizable story shares one underlying structure. It traces Joseph Campbell's **monomyth** (the Hero's Journey, from *The Hero with a Thousand Faces*) and Dan Harmon's distillation of it into the eight-step **Story Circle** — YOU, NEED, GO, SEARCH, FIND, TAKE, RETURN, CHANGE — then applies the circle beat-by-beat to *Star Wars: A New Hope*. Most of the framework is credited directly to Dan Harmon's own "Story Structure 101" breakdown.

> **Related:** This is the seed of a new **Narrative** category in the repo (story craft / narrative structure) — reference material for creative writing and the GrimGlow story bible.

---

## [0:02](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=2) Society Runs on Stories

- **Human society runs on stories.** They create our reality and the way we see the world — they make us sad, happy, and inspired.
- It's no surprise Hollywood, TV, and books **bring in hundreds of billions of dollars each year**, or that nearly the whole country rooted for the Cubs as eternal underdogs. Day in and day out, most people want to live the best story they can.
- The provocation: it *feels* like there are infinite, all-different stories — but the **basic structure of all of them is the same**. Not the style that makes them different — just the basic structure of anything recognizable as a story.

---

## [1:01](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=61) Joseph Campbell & the Monomyth (Hero's Journey)

- Many people have tried different formulas, but perhaps no one did it better than philosopher and theologian **Joseph Campbell**, who developed the **monomyth** — also known as the **Hero's Journey** — in his masterwork *The Hero with a Thousand Faces*.
- It wasn't a screenwriting book. Campbell grew up a Boy Scout and a Catholic, fascinated by Native American stories, and noticed **similarities between parables about Christ and Native American folktales** that predated Christ and had no contact with Christian culture. His life's work became **comparative mythology**.
- Mythology isn't just stories around a campfire — **"it's pop music, it's the dream you're describing to your friend on the subway, it's drawings on a napkin, it's basically everything."**
- After years of study, Campbell concluded the **characteristics of an effective story are consistent regardless of religion, race, time, or ancestry** — "nothing short of genius."

## [2:05](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=125) Dan Harmon Distills It: The Story Circle

- **Dan Harmon** simplified the monomyth into an even more basic structure that helps anyone build a story: **the Story Circle**, a distillation of the monomyth into **eight steps**.
- Harmon believes his circle is **universal for any story in any medium**.

---

## [2:27](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=147) The Theory: Ordinary World vs. Special World

- Start with the most basic form: a **circle with a horizontal line through it**.
- The **top** of the circle is where the character's journey **starts and finishes**. The **bottom** is the world that must be **traversed in order to grow and change**.
- In basic terms: the top is the **ordinary world**; the bottom is the **special world**.

---

## [2:46](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=166) Three Dualities (Life/Death, Conscious/Unconscious, Order/Chaos)

Harmon says every story has a rhythm or balance, explained by **three dualities**:

- **Life and death.** You're alive, then dead; your body decomposes and feeds plants, giving new life that then dies. "That's a story — it goes back and forth." The two rely on each other; **balance is needed for things to happen.**
- **Consciousness and unconsciousness.** Upstairs the conscious mind is comfortable, well-lit, and regularly swept; downstairs in the basement the unconscious is older, darker, and freakier — the stuff you don't want to or can't think about. Your pleasure, sanity, and life **depend on occasional round trips**: ego ventures into the unconscious via **therapy, meditation, confession, sex, violence, or a good story** keep the conscious in working order.
- **Order and chaos** (the third duality, made explicit later): just as an individual's health depends on the ego's descent and return, a **society's longevity depends on people journeying into the unknown and returning with ideas**. In their most dramatic form these people are **heroes**, but every day society is replenished by millions diving into darkness and emerging with something new or forgotten.

---

## [4:12](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=252) Descent & Return: Why Stories Resonate

- To Harmon, **all stories follow this pattern of descent and return, of diving and emerging.** All life — including the human mind and the communities we create — **marches to the same beat.**
- If a story marches to this beat, **it will resonate**: it sends the audience's ego on a brief trip to the unconscious and back.
- **"The audience has an instinctive taste for that, and they're going to say, 'Yum.'"**

---

## [4:36](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=276) Building the Circle: The Eight Steps

- Add a **vertical line** down the center, creating four intersections and four quadrants.
- Number the four crossing points (top, going clockwise): **1, 3, 5, 7**. Number the four quarter-sections themselves: **2, 4, 6, 8**. Each number carries a label that walks through the story piece by piece:

1. **YOU** — a character is in a zone of comfort.
2. **NEED** — they want something.
3. **GO** — they enter an unfamiliar situation.
4. **SEARCH** — adapt to it.
5. **FIND** — get what they wanted.
6. **TAKE** — pay a heavy price for it.
7. **RETURN** — then return to their familiar situation.
8. **CHANGE** — having changed.

---

## [5:31](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=331) The Two Semicircles: External vs. Inner Struggle

Crossing from one half to the other is a **major source of drama**:

- **Top → bottom (the horizontal line crossing):** the hero **enters a new situation and is forced to adapt**, usually struggling. This is the protagonist **fighting some external force** — the external struggle (ordinary world vs. special world).
- **Left → right (the vertical line crossing):** the **inner struggle**. Once the hero crosses this line, they finally face and try to **overcome their inner flaws or problems.**
- **Example — *Die Hard*:** top-to-bottom is a **failing marriage** and a **terrorist attack**; left-to-right is **stubborn / not stubborn**. Once McClane descends and returns, he is **no longer stubborn** and now has the power to fix his failing marriage.
- In simplest terms: **order / chaos, stasis / change.**

## [6:23](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=383) Applying the Circle to Star Wars: 1 — YOU

- **YOU — establish a protagonist.** When a story starts, the audience is "just loading, like a ghost" — you have to give them a place to land.
- How? **Just show a character.** Fade in on them, and **we are them until we have a better choice.** "Okay, let's go." (Luke Skywalker.)

---

## [6:58](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=418) 2 — NEED

- **NEED — something ain't quite right.** This is where we learn **something is off balance** in the universe, no matter how large or small that universe is.
- War between Earth and aliens → we see the ship coming toward Earth. A rom-com → the protagonist is on a terrible blind date. **Things aren't perfect and could be better** — this is the reason the story takes place.
- *Star Wars:* the discovery of the droids and Leia's message — **"You must learn the ways of the Force… if you were to come with me to Alderaan."**

---

## [7:43](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=463) 3 — GO (Crossing the Threshold)

- **GO — crossing the threshold.** The YOU was in a certain situation; now that situation **changes** — a terrorist attack occurs, a small-town girl leaves for the big city.
- The top half is the **ordinary world**, the bottom half the **special world**. Size doesn't matter, but **there must be contrast between the two worlds.**
- *Star Wars:* **"There's nothing for me here now. I want to learn the ways of the Force and become a Jedi like my father."**

---

## [8:21](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=501) 4 — SEARCH (The Road of Trials)

- **SEARCH — the road of trials.** The protagonist doesn't have to literally train; this is where they **adapt and experiment.**
- In *The Hero with a Thousand Faces*, Campbell uses the image of a **digestive tract** — breaking a person down, taking out certain neuroses, **stripping them of fear and desire.**
- **"Nothing can save you here"** — glasses, credit cards, promotion, cell phones. The purpose becomes refreshingly simple: we're headed to the **deepest level of the unconscious mind**, and we can't reach it surrounded by all the stuff we think is important.
- *Star Wars:* the escape from the Death Star's tractor beam / pursuit.

---

## [9:17](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=557) 5 — FIND (Meeting with the Goddess)

- **FIND — meeting with the goddess.** The job of the road of trials is to **prepare you for this meeting.** Campbell named it; Harmon kept the name.
- The hero **finds out what they're looking for, even if it isn't what they thought** they were looking for. This is the **big revelation**, the point of **greatest vulnerability** — a special pivot point where the protagonist hovers, **makes a choice, and descends.**
- The "goddess" can be **a gesture, an idea, a gun, a person, or a destination.** Whether or not it was the conscious goal, **the NEED from step 2 is fulfilled** — boy who wants adventure rescues princess, check.
- *Star Wars:* **"I'm Luke Skywalker. I'm here to rescue you."**

---

## [10:32](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=632) 6 — TAKE (Meet Your Maker)

- **TAKE — meet your maker.** The hardest part: you realize something is **really important, to the point where it's more important than you.** You've gained **full control over your destiny.**
- In the first half of the circle you **react** to the forces of the universe — adapting, changing, seeking. Now you've **become the universe** — that which makes things happen.
- Two sides of the same beat: **the price of the journey** (the shark eats the boat, you lose a friend) and **a goal achieved you never knew you had** (the shark now has an oxygen tank in its mouth; the lost friend has bought you time to complete the mission).

---

## [11:43](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=703) 7 — RETURN (Bringing It Home)

- **RETURN — bringing it home.** For some stories this is as simple as **waking up**; for others, someone must be **pulled out of an extreme situation** — the hero runs out of an apartment to stop a wedding, a huge car chase.
- **"It's not a journey if you never come back."**
- *Star Wars:* **"Analysis of the plans provided by Princess Leia has demonstrated a weakness in the battle station."**

---

## [12:17](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=737) 8 — CHANGE (Master of Both Worlds)

- **CHANGE — master of both worlds.** The YOU is **in charge of their situation again, but has now become a situation changer.** Life will never be the same.
- In an action film this is the **showdown** — the hero gets to the wedding in time to stop it.
- *Star Wars:* **this is when the Death Star is blown up.**

---

## [13:16](https://youtu.be/LuD2Aa0zFiA?t=796) Why It Works for Any Story — and Why It Matters

- The circle **applies to any piece of a story.** A sitcom writer (e.g., *Community*) writes **circles for each character** — how they traverse the story and change from top to bottom, left to right, as it moves forward.
- Is it the only way to make a story? **No** — but Harmon believes all stories come back to this structure, **otherwise they're not recognizable as stories.**
- Deconstructing storytelling this way shouldn't make it seem dull or repetitive. Harmon's own work is **proof that dedicated storytellers can use this basic structure to tell incredibly complex, subversive, and consistently meaningful stories in their own style.**
- Great stories — a meaningful conversation, your team winning a championship, a great book — **keep us psychologically, spiritually, and socially alive**, fine-tuning us to the rhythm of life and death, conscious and unconscious, the order and chaos of the world and how they work together. **"A great story can change someone's life. So get out there and tell yours."**

> **Closing idea:** Every story is the same descent-and-return at heart — Campbell's monomyth, distilled by Harmon into eight steps (YOU, NEED, GO, SEARCH, FIND, TAKE, RETURN, CHANGE). The top/bottom axis is the external struggle (ordinary vs. special world); the left/right axis is the inner struggle (order/chaos, stasis/change). Structure isn't a cage — it's the beat that lets a story resonate. "So get out there and tell yours."
